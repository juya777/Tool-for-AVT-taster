What do these technical constraints mean?

CPL: Characters per line, including punctuation and optionally spaces. Each line is counted separately.

CPS: Characters per second = all subtitle characters ÷ display duration; line breaks are not counted.

Maximum lines: How many lines a single subtitle may contain.

Line breaks: Use Enter to choose breaks; avoid splitting closely connected grammatical units. The tool checks line counts, not grammatical quality.

Duration: Time between a segment’s start and end timecodes.

Overlap: Two source segments occupy the same time; tutors validate this during setup.

Dubbing syllables/s: Estimated spoken syllables per second; a rough heuristic, not measured speech.

Isochrony/tolerance: How closely estimated translated speech duration fits the allotted time, within the tutor’s percentage allowance.

Technical constraints: explanations for tutors

Maximum lines: Permitted subtitle lines per cue. Maximum CPL: Characters on the longest line, including punctuation and (if selected) spaces.

Maximum CPS: Total characters, excluding line breaks, divided by the cue duration in seconds. Minimum/maximum duration: Allowed time on screen, calculated from imported start and end timecodes.

Line breaks: Students insert these manually; the tool checks length and number of lines, but cannot reliably judge grammatical segmentation.

Overlaps: The timecode validator checks whether consecutive cues overlap or are out of order.

Estimated dubbing syllables/s: Assumed speaking rate used to estimate delivery time. Dubbing tolerance: Allowed percentage difference between estimated delivery time and the allocated slot (isochrony).

Character count: Choose whether spaces count towards CPL/CPS. These are teaching settings, not universal standards; dubbing estimates are especially approximate across languages.

Preset values are editable starting points, not universal professional standards. Chinese characters and punctuation count as individual Unicode characters; syllable estimates are approximate, especially for non-English languages.
